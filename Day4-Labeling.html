|Interior Segmentation
Project Overview

Purpose of the Project

Label interior spaces with the polygon tool and classify every object into 42 classes. You view photos and tag objects to create the training dataset.

ToolClassesTips
Polygon42Label complex borders (such as trees) in as much detail as possible.
Label distant objects first: they may overlap with nearby objects.
Every single object must be labelled separately.

|Interior Segmentation
Class Rules

Use “undefined” Only When Nothing Fits

  • Do not tag a category as undefined if a matching class already exists.
  • Door: wooden doors, Turkish doors, etc.
  • Glass wall: building exterior walls, meeting room walls (not glass in partitions).
  • Glass door: all glass doors and frames connected to elevator spaces.
  • Signage: ceiling bar, vertical, or anything advertising something.
  • Furniture: partition includes glass partitions.
  • Appliance: floor vent / floor outlet: air holes and power holes, even when closed.
  • Appliance: vending machine: any vending machine type.

|Interior Segmentation
Quick Recap

Labelling Order

1
Raw data
Study the whole interior scene first.
2
Create polygons
One polygon per object, distant objects first, with no gaps between polygons.
3
Tag classes
Pick the right class out of 42 for every polygon.
4
Check colours
Shift + 2 / Shift + 3 show the colour mask, so gaps stand out.
Be careful not to leave any spaces between polygons.

|Photobooth Project
Quick Recap

Photobooth Rules at a Glance

  • Face box: head, face and neck inside the box (neck up to the forehead).
  • Hair, hats, headbands and bags are not part of the face box.
  • Body polygon: the whole-body outline of every person.
  • Hair, bags, hats and headbands are part of the body polygon.
  • Set gender, age and image brightness for each face.
  • Every person in the image is a labelling target.
Age bands: under 10 · teen · 20s · 30s · 40 and above. The next slides show examples of each.
