Bounding Box Basics

What Is a Bounding Box?

A bounding box is an imaginary rectangle that serves as a point of reference for object detection and creates a collision box for that object in image-processing projects.

  • Drawn tightly around the object, with edges touching its outermost pixels.
  • Each box gets a category (class) so the AI learns what the object is.
  • Fast to draw and used in a huge range of real projects.

Categories (Classes)

These are the objects you want the AI model to recognise. They are organised into categories, also known as classes, which may have different states or subcategories depending on the project. A project can have anywhere from 1 to 12 or more classes.

Quick Recap

Bounding Box Essentials

Draw
Click and drag diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner.
D Delete
Click the box to select it, then press D, or press D right after drawing.
Move & Zoom
Hold click and drag to move a box. Use the zoom buttons to check the edges.
L Labels
Turn class labels on or off while labelling.
B Borders
Turn box borders on or off to see the object underneath.
G Guideline
Turn the white cross guideline on or off for precise corners.

1. Hold Your Pen Vertically

Keep the stylus upright rather than laid flat. It gives you finer control over the tip.

|CCTV Project
Project Overview

Purpose of the Project

Purpose
A project to detect and encrypt personal information in CCTV footage, such as human faces and vehicle licence plates.
Box the Face
The face is a labelling target and the ears are included. The back of a head is not a target. Faces that are tiny or unidentifiable are still targets.
Box the Licence Plate
Include all letters and numbers. Label plates even if the characters are unreadable, as long as the plate edges are visible. Motorcycle plates are targets.
|CCTV Project
Labelling Target

Labelling Criteria at a Glance

  • Only the face is the target, not the hair.
  • Masks and glasses on the face are included.
  • Earplugs, scarves, bags, umbrellas, helmets, hats and handheld accessories are not targets.
  • If earplugs or a phone cover the face, estimate the face range and label it.
  • The back of a person / back or top of the head without a face is not a target.
  • Faces reflected in glass, a mirror or an elevator wall are targets.
  • For side faces, include the ears.
  • No person in the image? Press Skip without labelling.
This is a face labelling project. When in doubt, ask: is there a face here?
