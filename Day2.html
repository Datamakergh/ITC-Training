Polyline Basics

What Is Polyline Annotation?

Polyline annotation is used to annotate long, narrow shapes in an image. You place a series of points along the object and connect them to follow its path accurately.

  • Marking a road lane: points are placed along the lane and joined to match its direction.
  • Useful for road lanes, railway tracks and pipelines.

Source: Polyline Annotation and Automated Vehicles, keymakr.com

Quick Recap

Traffic Flow Rules at a Glance

  • Label every lane with a type (solid, dotted, stop-line) and a colour.
  • Label each line of a double solid line separately.
  • A U-turn line is a white, dotted line.
  • Lanes hidden by fences or cars: label what the lane originally was.
  • Do not label road leaders, bus parking lines, crossings, bike lanes or road text.
  • Parking on the road: label the vertical line only.
  • Discard tasks with no target, a flipped image, or only dim, broken lanes.
  • Outermost and Y-shaped lanes are usually solid yellow.

Exact outlines for objects with irregular or complex shapes

|Polygon Basics
Polygon Basics

What Is Polygon Annotation?

Polygon annotation marks an object by clicking to create points around its edges. Connected, the points form a shape that closely follows the real outline of the object.

  • Unlike a rectangle, a polygon can change direction and create different angles.
  • Ideal for objects with irregular or complex shapes.

Precaution

Common Rules

  1. 1Label with one bounding box per object.
  2. 2Annotations are tagged for each end of the object, and multiple annotations can overlap.
  3. 3If part of the object is invisible or occluded, label the visible part only.
  4. 4Label the object as far as it is visible, regardless of its visible range.
Choose normal or abnormal for every object based on its condition.
